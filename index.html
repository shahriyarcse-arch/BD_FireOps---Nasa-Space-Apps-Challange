EARTH OBSERVATION · CROSS-SENSOR HARMONIZATION

One Continuous Fire Record for 20 Years of NASA Satellite Data

Two NASA satellite instruments have monitored forest and agricultural burning in the Chittagong Hill Tracts: Aqua MODIS (2002–2021, 1 km pixels) and S-NPP VIIRS (2012–2021, 375 m pixels). Because VIIRS sees small fires that MODIS missed, joining the two cameras directly makes it look like fires almost tripled overnight (+185%). BD-FireOps rebuilds the record on one footing and, just as importantly, says plainly how much of that repair is simple scaling and how much is ours. It is a calibration for these three districts with a measured confidence range and a real held-out test, not a universal MODIS-to-VIIRS conversion.

STEP 01 · WHY THE JOIN LOOKS WRONG

Wrong Join vs BD-FireOps Harmonized Splice

If we join the two cameras directly, fires look almost 2.85× higher after 2012 — but only the camera changed, not the forest.

The Formula & How It Was Trained

Fitted across 84 overlapping months (2012–2018) when both Aqua MODIS and S-NPP VIIRS orbited simultaneously:

MODIS-Equivalent = 0.2694 × VIIRS − 1.18

Parameter 95% Confidence Interval from 500 moving-block bootstrap resamples: Slope [0.2198, 0.2970], Intercept [-6.79, +6.52]. Validated against an unseen 3-year test horizon (2019–2021).

Reverse direction, for carrying the record forward after Suomi-NPP delivery ends on 1 November 2026: VIIRS-Equivalent = 3.7126 × MODIS + 4.37. This is an algebraic inversion of the same line, not a model fitted on NOAA-20 detections, so a NOAA-20 leg must be fitted on the S-NPP / NOAA-20 overlap before the forward series is used operationally.

The model was trained exclusively on 2012–2018. We hid the final 3 years (36 months) completely, then benchmarked our predictions against ground-truth Aqua MODIS observations.

R² is 0.9811, but it is set mostly by the burning peak, so rank agreement is the fairer single number.

Read this honestly: most of the correction comes from simple scaling, not from our equation. An unscaled splice scores RMSE 1130.64, bias +468.92 and R² -6.8012, so measured against it the error falls 95.1% and the bias falls 99.1%. That is the wrong yardstick. Measured against a one-number scaling baseline, the error falls only 1.7%. What this project adds is the regional calibration for the CHT, the bootstrap confidence range, the confidence-threshold and day-only sensitivity runs, and a genuine held-out test.

Data source: NASA FIRMS Area API (MODIS_SP, VIIRS_SNPP_SP) over the CHT window — recorded in data/processed/data_source.json. Detections restricted to CHT districts (Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati) polygons (geoBoundaries BGD-ADM2); 54,300 MODIS and 85,857 VIIRS rows outside the CHT dropped. Learned on 2012-01 to 2018-12 (84 months), tested on 2019-01 to 2021-12 (36 months). Full formula & confidence intervals: see the “Wrong Join” section above.
Confidence-threshold sensitivity: keeping low-confidence detections moves the slope by -8.54% and increases held-out RMSE by 4.96% — baseline thresholds are reported.

HONEST SCIENTIFIC LIMITS

What BD-FireOps Is and Is Not

Where this tool stops — stated plainly.

  • Not a Live Fire Alarm: This system harmonizes historical climate and environmental records for 2002–2021. It is not an active real-time dispatch feed.
  • Region-Specific Scope: Empirically calibrated specifically for the 3 hill districts of Bangladesh (Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari). Applying these coefficients to boreal or tropical rainforest biomes requires local recalibration.
  • Detection Realities: NASA FIRMS reports positive thermal detections. Absence of detection does not guarantee absence of ground fire; heavy monsoon cloud cover (June–October) or satellite orbital overpass gaps can obscure active burning.
  • No Fire-Cause Attribution: BD-FireOps standardizes fire observation numbers; it does not classify whether a thermal anomaly was intentional Jhum agricultural clearing or accidental forest wildfire.
  • Operational Succession: Suomi-NPP VIIRS delivery officially terminates on November 1, 2026. Future pipelines must extend this methodology to NOAA-20 (VJ114) and NOAA-21 (VJ214).
OPEN SOURCE · STEP BY STEP

Five Steps, Completely Open Source

Every data point, script, and test is version-controlled and reproducible.

  1. Primary Archive Download: Downloaded official NASA FIRMS thermal anomaly archives (MODIS Collection 6.1 and VIIRS S-NPP 375 m) spanning 2002–2021.
  2. Quality & Polygon Filtering: Restricted detections strictly to the Chittagong Hill Tracts district polygons (geoBoundaries BGD-ADM2), discarding border-box noise and low-confidence detections. Filtered MODIS to afternoon Aqua overpasses for orbit-time alignment.
  3. Monthly Aggregation & Zero-Month Preservation: Preserved true 0 counts during sensor coverage while distinguishing unobserved months as null.
  4. Overlap Training & Blind Testing: Trained the linear transfer model on 2012–2018 (84 overlapping months) and evaluated blind on 2019–2021 (36 held-out months). Evaluated parameter stability with 500 moving-block bootstrap resamples.
  5. Automated Sync & CI Guards: Automated continuous-integration pipeline (sync_web.py, sync_readme.py) guaranteeing zero drift between model parameters and presentation surfaces.
