NASA SPACE APPS 2026 · CHITTAGONG HILL TRACTS

Hill-Tracts fires did not triple in 2012. The satellite camera changed.

Two NASA satellites have been counting fires in the Chittagong Hill Tracts since 2002. In 2012 a sharper one joined, and the count almost tripled overnight. The forest didn't change. The camera did. BD-FireOps puts both satellites on the same scale, so anyone can see whether a season was really unusual, and what NOAA-20 is picking up this week.

In one line: the satellite that never changed (Aqua MODIS) actually saw less burning after 2012, about 23% less. The jump comes from the new camera, not from the hills.

    When do the Hills burn?

    An average year, built from 20 years of satellite records. Almost all of it happens in two months.

    • Before the season (Jan–Feb): clear dry litter near homes, granaries and schools. মৌসুমের আগে: ঘর, গোলা ও স্কুলের পাশের শুকনো পাতা-আবর্জনা সরিয়ে ফেলুন।
    • During jhum burning (Mar–Apr): burn only on calm days, keep a cleared firebreak, and never leave a burn unattended. জুম পোড়ানোর সময়: বাতাস কম এমন দিনে পোড়ান, চারপাশে ফাঁকা অগ্নিরোধক পথ রাখুন, আর আগুন কখনো অরক্ষিত রাখবেন না।

    Why you can trust it

    Why we say the 2012 jump comes from the camera, and how we tested that on years the model had never seen.

    TRUST IT · WHY THE JOIN LOOKS WRONG

    The 2012 jump that isn't real

    Join the two satellites' counts directly and fires look almost 2.85× higher after 2012. Only the camera changed. Drag the handle to compare the raw join with our corrected record.

    The formula, and how we fitted it

    Fitted on the 84 overlapping months (2012-01 to 2018-12) when Aqua MODIS and S-NPP VIIRS both watched the Hill Tracts:

    MODIS-equivalent = 0.2694 × VIIRS − 1.18

    95% range from 500 moving-block bootstrap re-runs: slope [0.2198, 0.297], intercept [-6.79, +6.52]. Checked on held-out years 2019-01 to 2021-12.

    The same line run backwards, to keep the record going after Suomi-NPP stopped on 1 November 2026: VIIRS-equivalent = 3.7126 × MODIS + 4.37. This is arithmetic on the same line, not a model fitted on NOAA-20 data, so it should not be used on its own until a NOAA-20 leg has been fitted on real overlap months.

    R² is 0.9811, but it is set mostly by the burning peak, so rank agreement is the fairer single number.

    Worth knowing: most of the correction comes from simple scaling, not from our equation. A raw join (no correction) scores RMSE 1130.64, bias +468.92 and R² -6.8012, so measured against it the error falls 95.1% and the bias falls 99.1%. That is the wrong yardstick. Measured against a one-number scaling baseline, the error falls only 1.7%. What this project adds is the regional calibration for the CHT, the bootstrap confidence range, the confidence-threshold and day-only sensitivity runs, and a genuine held-out test.

    How it works and where it stops

    The method, the words we use, and what the tool can't do.

    UNDERSTAND IT · PLAIN WORDS

    The words we use

    Hotspot (detection)
    A satellite pixel that was unusually hot when the satellite passed over. Usually a fire, but not a confirmed fire event and not burned area.
    MODIS / VIIRS
    Two NASA satellite cameras. MODIS sees 1 km squares; VIIRS sees 375 m squares, so it catches smaller fires.
    Harmonized
    VIIRS counts converted to what MODIS would have counted, so the whole 20 years sits on one scale.
    Held-out test
    We fitted the conversion on 2012–2018 only, then checked it on 2019–2021, data the model never saw.
    Monthly error (RMSE)
    The typical size of a monthly miss, in hotspots. Large months dominate it.
    Rank agreement (Spearman)
    Whether busy and quiet months come out in the right order. 1.0 is perfect.
    95% range
    How much the conversion factor could shift if the data had come out slightly differently (bootstrap). It is a range on the factor, not on a single month.
    Jhum
    Traditional shifting cultivation in the Hill Tracts; fields are cleared by fire mostly in March–April.
    UNDERSTAND IT · OPEN METHOD

    How it works

    All the code is public, and the whole thing re-runs from NASA data with one command.

    1. Download. We pulled every NASA FIRMS fire detection for the area from Aqua MODIS (2002 to 2021) and Suomi-NPP VIIRS (2012 to 2021).
    2. Clean. We kept only points inside the three district boundaries, dropped low-confidence detections, and used only the afternoon Aqua passes so both satellites look at the same time of day.
    3. Count by month. A month with no fire counts as 0. A month with no satellite data stays empty, so it is never mistaken for a quiet month.
    4. Fit and test. We fitted the MODIS–VIIRS line on 2012 to 2018, tested it on 2019 to 2021, and re-ran the fit 500 times to see how much it moves.
    5. Keep the page honest. Every number you see is written into the page by a script from our results files, and an automated check fails if anything doesn't match.
    6. Check for new data. Every 6 hours a scheduled job asks NASA FIRMS for the last 5 days of NOAA-20/21 detections and updates the "Right now" chapter.
    UNDERSTAND IT · HONEST LIMITS

    What this tool can and can't do

    Please read this before you use the numbers.

    • It is not a fire alarm. The "Right now" chapter shows a near-real-time NOAA-20/21 snapshot that we refresh every 6 hours. It is provisional and can be hours old. In an emergency, call the Fire Service.
    • It only covers three districts. The correction was fitted for Bandarban, Rangamati and Khagrachhari. Somewhere with different forests or farming would need its own fit.
    • No dot doesn't mean no fire. Satellites only see heat when they pass over and when clouds allow. Monsoon cloud (June to October) hides a lot.
    • It doesn't know why something burned. A jhum field, a forest fire and a brick kiln can all show up as a hotspot. We count them; we don't tell them apart.
    • The record after 2021 is not finished. Suomi-NPP stopped on 1 November 2026. Continuing the 20-year record needs a NOAA-20 fit on real overlap months, which we have not done yet.
